# MacroBay 0.4.4

What changed for the people using the app, and what to look at before you upgrade a copy you already
run. For what the app can and cannot do at all, see [Status and limitations](../status-and-limitations.md).

## Download

- macOS · Apple Silicon: `MacroBay-arm64.dmg`
- macOS · Intel: `MacroBay-x64.dmg`

**Intel build status.** The Intel build is `MacroBay-x64.dmg`: it is built by the release workflow and
ships with this version, but **its first install has not been completed on Intel hardware yet**. The
Apple Silicon download link is unchanged, and `latest-mac.yml` is produced only by the arm64 job.

The build is unsigned and not notarized, so the first launch still takes the steps in item 1 below.
Dragging the app, right-clicking to open, and what to do when there is no Open item are all written in
the repository's "Download and install" page.

## Read this before upgrading

1. **This build is neither code-signed nor notarized.** The first launch takes two steps: on macOS, drag
   the app into Applications, then **right-click (or Control-click) → Open** and confirm — a plain
   double-click is stopped by Gatekeeper; if the menu has no Open item (newer macOS versions removed
   it), go to **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**. On Windows, run the
   installer and choose **More info → Run anyway** in SmartScreen. **Both steps are needed again after
   every downloaded update.**
2. **One message can now go to several channels at once.** The previous version could enable only one
   channel at a time; this one can enable several, each channel gets its own copy and its own result,
   and one failing does not stop the others.
3. **The official calendar from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics is still unreachable** (the endpoint
   returns 403), so automatic sync stays off. What is already in the calendar is frozen at the last
   successful sync, and those events carry no publication time.
4. **Two platform-side switches are prerequisites, as before.** For Discord, open **Message Content** for
   this bot in the developer portal first, or the gateway refuses the connection. For Slack, turn on
   **Socket Mode** on the app side. Both live in the platform's backend, not in the app.

## What you will see

### News sentiment: ask for an interpretation of one story

- **The news right-click menu grew "AI interpretation"**: ask for one on that story on demand, instead
  of waiting for it to be picked automatically.
- **A per-story AI interpretation is started only by you, and the result stays in that story's detail**:
  it never appears in the latest-conclusion card, the analysis timeline, or the daily briefing.
- **A story has at most one interpretation**: once one exists the menu says "View AI interpretation"
  instead of starting a second one.
- **When the interpretation cannot be read, the menu says so in the item itself ("This interpretation
  cannot be read right now"); while it is being generated, the item is only greyed out and carries no
  reason.** No interpretation yet reads "AI interpretation", one that exists reads "View AI
  interpretation", and one whose evidence has changed reads "Interpret again".
- **A story that is neutral, names no asset and has no direct impact is no longer treated as a
  high-importance event to analyse.** Such stories are marked on the dashboard only.
- **An interpretation no longer sits at "interpreting" forever.** One that produces nothing for a long
  time settles as a failure with "Try again"; closing and reopening the app also settles the stuck one
  as a failure instead of leaving it spinning.

### News sentiment: five strength levels, desktop notifications from Medium up

- **News strength has five levels**: 1 unrelated to crypto, 2 inside crypto but a single asset with no
  spillover, 3 moves overall risk appetite or a whole asset class, 4 moves BTC/ETH flows or supply
  directly, 5 a shock that may change the trend. The scale applies to new stories only — **earlier
  judgements are not re-scored**.
- **The desktop notification threshold is Medium** (the middle of three levels), and nothing is sent
  during quiet hours.
- **"Not judged yet" is not "low"**: an unjudged story does not show up in the news list, does not raise
  a desktop notification, and cannot be interpreted.

### Channels: one message goes to every enabled channel

- **With several channels enabled, one message is sent to each**, and every channel has its own result
  and its own failure reason; one dropping out does not stop the rest.
- **The receipt is read per send**: a manual send used to report "kept in the app, did not reach your
  phone" because the receipt was thrown away; it now says which channel received it and why another did
  not.
- **Telegram, Slack and Discord can now receive images**: the daily briefing's "publish to channel" sends
  the poster plus an introduction; text-only channels get the introduction and are not dressed up as if
  they could take an image.
- **When no channel is ready, the settings page no longer tells you to switch off until one is left**:
  "nothing enabled" and "enabled but not connected" are two different situations, and are now described
  separately.

### Briefing and analysis: every number cites a source

- **The number-citation check grew from the conclusion to every section that prints numbers**: a
  percentage in the scenarios, transmission, catalysts or direction reasoning that the snapshot does not
  carry now rejects the whole analysis, instead of handing you something that reads well but does not
  match its evidence.
- **"What would invalidate this" is no longer free text**: it now cites a fact or a moment from the
  snapshot, validation compares data only and never reads the sentence, and older records keep showing
  as they were.
- **One briefing now carries one time point**: the same briefing used to print two sets of numbers side
  by side (one from a previous analysis, one from the day's window); the body now keeps only the day's.
- **The briefing grew a line for "what would overturn this call"**: this line used to be always empty; it
  now carries the condition, on the page and in the exported text.
- **The analysis timeline is split into a "succeeded" and a "failed" tab**: failed runs no longer mix into
  the published records, so finding why one run produced nothing does not mean scrolling past the rest.
- **A run's quota is counted per run, and a failure counts**: the worst-case number of calls is written
  into the sentence shown when the quota runs out, and you can see how many calls were made today.

### Markets and funds

- **The quote card now shows the window's range**, the high and the low of the window, on the card
  itself instead of leaving you to work it out.
- **The funds net-flow chart keeps a fixed window**: when today has no reading yet, today is not drawn
  and the window moves back one day, still covering the same number of days; a past day without data
  keeps an empty slot — it is neither filled with 0 (0 is a real reading) nor removed from the axis.
- **ETF net flow moved to a steadier source**: the previous direct call was blocked often and could drop
  out at any time; collection, the dashboard and the briefing now all read the new source.

### Calendar

- **The calendar is a drawer now, not a whole page**: the calendar icon on a page opens it over that page
  instead of navigating away, and closing it returns you where you were.
- **Calendar notifications can be limited by country**: only the U.S., all countries, or a set you pick.

### Sources and settings

- **The two collection-frequency steps now actually work**: "balanced" and "frequent" used to collect at
  the same pace; they now use their own intervals, and the real interval is written in the question mark
  on that row.
- **The frequency row is no longer cut off by an ellipsis**: the interval and the next reading moved into
  the question mark beside it, leaving "Frequency ⓘ + dropdown" in the row, and the numbers inside change
  when you change the step.
- **The four core indicators list their sources**: each one names which sources feed it, and each leg can
  be switched off on its own.
- **The "website" button on a source's detail is drawn from data**: it appears only when the source has a
  website, and when that is the same as the registration address the two collapse into one.
- **The "VPN" tag follows the measurement**: a source that needs no proxy in testing no longer carries the
  tag.
- **The storage page lists quarantined corrupt copies**: files isolated on disk used to be invisible; they
  are listed now, with what they are, whether they can be deleted, and what deleting them means.

### When something goes wrong

- **A stuck read no longer spins forever**: a read past its deadline settles as a failure with a retry,
  so "give it more time" and "it is broken" stop looking the same.
- **The window appears sooner at startup**: less white screen before the interface, which now draws its
  frame first and fills it in after.

## Known limitations

- **Neither the arm64 nor the Windows installer has completed a real install check.** Someone installed
  and used the arm64 build on an Apple silicon machine during 0.4.0, and it has not been re-verified by a
  real install since; the Windows installer builds, but the installed app has no acceptance record.
- **The dollar index can be missing for whole stretches; that is a plan limit, not a fault.** The provider
  does not serve this series on the plan you have, so the line keeps a gap. After a plan that covers it is
  active, the 24-hour window fills back in over about a day, and the gap itself is not backfilled.
- **"No data in this stretch" now tells two causes apart**: either the market was closed, or the provider's
  plan does not serve that series — the second case is labelled "No data · not in plan" right in the legend.
- **The official calendar from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics is unreachable** (the endpoint returns
  403); see [Status and limitations](../status-and-limitations.md).
- **Lark and QQ send text only in this version**: image outbound is on for Telegram, Slack and Discord.
- **The analysis timeline lists runs that have a result or have failed**: a run that is still going is not
  in the timeline yet; it appears when it settles.
- **The briefing's macro section prints nothing when there really is no high-impact event**: that is
  deliberate, and the empty period is recorded in the "data gaps and limitations" column as "nothing to do".
- **An event is announced once; an upgrade in severity is not pushed again**: a story already sent to your
  phone as "medium" is not sent a second time when later coverage calls it "high".
- **There is no auto-update channel**: the version notice only tells you a new version exists; downloading
  and installing are still yours to do.
- **A message you send from your phone lands as an ordinary conversation**: named after the channel plus
  "chat" — "Lark chat", "QQ chat" — and opening it in the conversation list shows your message and the
  reply.
